You are producing visual REFERENCE SHEETS for "Greenleaf", a 3D Legolas fan game (private repo vicciz-ceo/legolas-greenleaf).
Every character in the game is modelled procedurally in TypeScript (signed-distance-field sculpting plus auto-skinning). Other agents tune those coded characters against your references, so they must be precise, consistent and measurable, not just pretty.

## Ground rules
- Work on a NEW branch `refs/character-sheets` created from `origin/build/foundation`. Add files ONLY under `docs/refs/`. Do not touch `src/`, `lab/`, `scripts/` or any existing file. Other agents are actively editing those.
- These images are development references. They are never imported by the game; the game itself stays "everything built in code".
- Original designs only. Follow Tolkien's descriptions and the general costume language of the Hobbit/LOTR film era, but do NOT reproduce any actor's likeness (Orlando Bloom, Viggo Mortensen, John Rhys-Davies, Evangeline Lilly, Lee Pace, etc.) or copy specific film frames or stills. Faces must be original people who fit the description.
- Before starting, read `GOAL.md`, `ARCHITECTURE.md` (§4 visual standard; the scale list) and `docs/CHAPTERS.md` for context.

## Deliverables per character: folder `docs/refs/<id>/`
1. `<id>_turnaround.png` (1536×1024): FOUR full-body views side by side at IDENTICAL scale: front, 3/4 front, right side profile, back.
   - Relaxed A-pose (arms ~30° from the body), feet shoulder-width apart, neutral expression.
   - Orthographic-feeling camera (no wide-angle distortion), eye level at mid-height.
   - A thin vertical height ruler on the left marked in 0.25 m steps with the total height labelled.
2. `<id>_face.png` (1536×1024): head and shoulders in front, 3/4 and exact profile. Show eye shape and lids, brow, nose bridge, lips, ears (pointed for elves), hairline and the hair silhouette.
3. `<id>_details.png` (1024×1024): a 2×2 or 3×3 grid of material close-ups: fabric weave, leather with stitching and edge wear, metal (scratches, rust, engraving), skin, hair strands, plus the signature weapon. Each tile carries a short label.
4. `spec.json`: machine-readable measurements and materials (schema below). Every number must match the images.
5. `notes.md`: 5–10 bullet "modelling notes": the silhouette features that make the character instantly readable, the proportions that matter most, and common mistakes to avoid.

## Image style (same for every sheet)
Photoreal concept-art render, as if from a film costume department's character bible:
- neutral mid-grey seamless background (#7f7f7f)
- soft, even studio key light from the upper front-left plus a gentle rim light, with no dramatic shadows and no colour cast
- crisp focus, true-to-life materials and colours
- no text other than the labels and ruler, no logos, no watermarks, no props other than the character's own gear

The same person and outfit must be consistent across all views and sheets. Regenerate whenever the views disagree, hands have the wrong number of fingers, or gear changes between views.

Base prompt template, used for every view (fill the brackets):

Character: [DESCRIPTION]. Height [H] m. Original face, not resembling any real actor."

## 1. Build the tooling first: `docs/refs/tools/compose.py` (Pillow + numpy; rembg optional)
Subcommands:
- `segment <in> <out>`: remove the background into an RGBA cutout. Use rembg if it is installed; otherwise use a colour-distance threshold from the corner median plus a morphological cleanup.
- `turnaround <id>`: read docs/refs/<id>/views/{front,three_quarter,side,back}.png cutouts and docs/refs/<id>/spec.json.
  - Scale every view so its silhouette height in pixels equals `overall_height_m × px_per_m`. Choose px_per_m so the tallest view fills 85% of 1024 px.
  - Put all feet on one baseline and composite onto an exact #7f7f7f 1536×1024 canvas.
  - DRAW the height ruler (0.25 m ticks, labelled) and view labels with PIL.
  - Write <id>_turnaround.jpg and record px_per_m and the view bounding boxes in spec.json under "measured".
- `face <id>`: compose the face views (front, 3/4, profile) onto an exact 1536×1024 #7f7f7f sheet with drawn labels. No scale requirement.
- `grid <id>`: compose individually generated detail tiles into an exact 1024×1024 2×2 or 3×3 grid with drawn labels.
- `sample <id>`: median sRGB over named rectangles (skin, hair, each garment, each metal) and write them to spec.json under "observed", with the rectangles' coordinates. Mark them "rendered and lit, not albedo".
- `preview64 <id>`: 64 px-high silhouette previews (temporary, not committed).
- `check <id>`: assert every by-construction property: canvas sizes, background value, ruler endpoints matching px_per_m, a shared baseline, and file size budgets. Exit non-zero on failure.
Never mirror an image to make another view (it flips handedness).

## 2. Generation rules (one view per generation)
- Generate the FRONT view first. Generate 3/4, side and back by editing or conditioning on the accepted front image ("same character, same outfit and gear, rotated to …").
- No text, rulers, labels, numbers, logos or watermarks in any generated image.
- Use a plain light-grey studio backdrop with soft, even lighting; the script replaces it afterwards.
- Relaxed A-pose. NOTHING may protrude above the top of the head (hold the bow and spears low), so the silhouette top is the head or headgear.
- Up to 4 attempts per view. If one character still fails, record why in QUALITY_REPORT.md and MOVE ON; do not let one character block the roster.

## 3. Quality gate (redefined)
MUST pass, judged by eye; reject if not:
- matches the character description; an original face (no actor likeness)
- correct anatomy (five fingers where visible, no fused or extra limbs); correct view angle
- the same gear in every view, on the same body side per the attachment table below
- no generated text; silhouette readable at 64 px
BY CONSTRUCTION, asserted by `compose.py check` and never judged by eye: canvas sizes, background colour, scale, ruler, labels, baseline.
TOLERATED (note it in notes.md; do not reject): small costume drift between views (buckle shape, stitch pattern, strap count), A-pose angle anywhere from 20 to 40°, mild lighting differences.
